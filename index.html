Research

Case Studies on Sunscreen

Key national and international research studies examining sunscreen safety, efficacy, environmental impact, and public health implications.

🇮🇳 National — India
Sunscreen Usage Patterns in Urban India: Mumbai Cohort Study (2022)
A cross-sectional study of 1,200 adults in Mumbai found that only 28% applied sunscreen daily despite India's UV index consistently exceeding 8–11. Key barriers: white cast from mineral sunscreens, cost sensitivity, and lack of UVA protection awareness. The study highlighted a mismatch between SPF 30 formulations widely sold and SPF 50+ recommended for Indian skin phototypes III–V.
Year2022
RegionMumbai, India
Sample1,200 adults
🇮🇳 National — India
Oxybenzone & Octinoxate Prevalence in Indian Cosmetic Sunscreens (CDSCO Review, 2021)
India's CDSCO assessed 30 Indian sunscreen formulations. Over 60% contained Oxybenzone (BP-3) and/or OMC — both classified as endocrine-disrupting chemicals by the European Chemicals Agency. The report called for alignment with EU Regulation No. 1223/2009 and recommended phasing out these ingredients in favour of next-gen filters like Tinosorb S and DHHB.
Year2021
BodyCDSCO, India
Scope30 products
🇮🇳 National — India
Sunscreen Photoprotection and Melanoma Incidence in South Indian Patients (AIIMS, 2020)
AIIMS New Delhi investigated sunscreen use and melanoma/hyperpigmentation outcomes in 400 dermatology patients. Consistent SPF 50+ use correlated with a 34% lower incidence of actinic keratosis and significant reduction in post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation. Most budget-range sunscreens lacked adequate UVA protection (PA++ or less), contributing to uneven skin tone despite UV protection claims.
Year2020
InstitutionAIIMS, New Delhi
Patients400
🇮🇳 National — India
Heavy Metal Contamination in Indian Sunscreens — IIT Bombay Analysis (2023)
Researchers at IIT Bombay tested 20 commercially available Indian sunscreens for heavy metal contamination using ICP-MS analysis. Alarming concentrations of lead (up to 4 ppm) and arsenic (up to 1.2 ppm) were detected in 3 budget formulations from unregulated supply chains. The study advocated for pharmaceutical-grade mineral filter sourcing and stricter quality standards for children's sunscreens.
Year2023
InstitutionIIT Bombay
MethodICP-MS
🌍 International — USA
FDA Sunscreen Active Ingredient Safety Study (JAMA, 2019)
A landmark randomised clinical trial in JAMA demonstrated that Avobenzone, Oxybenzone, Octocrylene, and Ecamsule were absorbed systemically at concentrations exceeding the FDA's 0.5 ng/mL safety threshold. Oxybenzone reached plasma concentrations up to 209.6 ng/mL after 4 days — over 400 times the benchmark. This prompted the FDA's 2021 proposal to reclassify 12 chemical filters to pending GRASE status.
Year2019
JournalJAMA
ImpactFDA Policy Change
🌍 International — Hawaii
Hawaii Sunscreen Ban: Coral Reef Impact of Oxybenzone & Octinoxate (2018–2021)
Hawaii became the first US state to ban sunscreens containing Oxybenzone and Octinoxate, effective January 1, 2021. Research by Dr. Craig Downs showed Oxybenzone at concentrations as low as 62 parts per trillion caused coral bleaching, DNA damage in juvenile coral, and endocrine disruption in marine organisms — directly relevant to Indian coastal zones including Goa, Andaman Islands, and Kerala.
Year2018–2021
RegionHawaii, USA
OutcomeLegislative Ban
🌍 International — Europe
SCCS Assessment of Benzophenone-3 Safety — EU Scientific Committee (2021)
The Scientific Committee on Consumer Safety (SCCS) concluded that Benzophenone-3 is not safe above 2.2% in leave-on products, down from the previously permitted 10%. The assessment cited endocrine disruption potential, systemic absorption, and phototoxicity. This led European formulators to rapidly adopt Tinosorb S, Bemotrizinol, and DHHB as safer broad-spectrum replacements.
Year2021
BodySCCS / EU
ResultRegulatory Limit Cut
🔬 Clinical — Global
Tinosorb S vs Avobenzone: Photostability Comparative Study (Cosmetics & Toiletries, 2020)
Bemotrizinol (Tinosorb S) retained 95% of its UV-absorbing capacity after 8 hours of simulated solar radiation, compared to only 36% for Avobenzone when used alone. Combined with photostabilisers, Avobenzone retention improved to ~75%, still significantly below Tinosorb S — reinforcing the move by next-gen formulations (Minimalist, Cetaphil, Pilgrim in this dataset) towards EU-approved filters.
Year2020
JournalCosmetics & Toiletries
FindingTinosorb S superior
Manufacturing

How Sunscreens Are Produced

The industrial and laboratory-scale method of preparation for modern sunscreen formulations — from raw UV filter synthesis to final product testing.

01
🔬
UV Filter Selection & Sourcing
Chemical UV filters (Tinosorb S, DHHB, Octinoxate) or physical filters (ZnO, TiO₂) are selected based on target SPF, UVA/UVB coverage, and regulatory approvals. Purity >99% is required. Physical filters are milled to particle size specifications (non-nano: >100nm).
02
⚗️
Phase Preparation
Oil Phase: UV filters, emollients (Dimethicone, C12-15 Alkyl Benzoate), waxes, and lipid-soluble actives dissolved together at 70–80°C. Water Phase: Humectants (Glycerin, Hyaluronic Acid), chelating agents (EDTA), and water-soluble actives separately dissolved and heated to matching temperature.
03
🌀
Emulsification
The water phase is slowly added to the oil phase (O/W emulsion) under high-shear homogenisation at 3,000–5,000 rpm. Emulsifiers (Glyceryl Stearate, PEG-100 Stearate) stabilise the blend. For gel formulations, carbomers or acrylate copolymers are used instead.
04
🌡️
Cooling & Active Integration
The emulsion is cooled to 40°C under slow mixing. Heat-sensitive actives are added: Niacinamide, Vitamin C derivatives, botanical extracts (Aloe Vera, Green Tea EGCG), and preservatives (Phenoxyethanol ≤1%). pH is adjusted to 5.5–6.5 using Citric Acid or Sodium Hydroxide.
05
📊
SPF & UVA Testing
SPF is determined via in vitro (spectrophotometric) and in vivo (ISO 24444) methods. UVA protection verified through Critical Wavelength (≥370nm), PA++ rating (JCIA Japan method), or Boot Star rating. Photostability testing (ISO 24443) ensures UV filter integrity after solar radiation exposure.
06
🧪
Safety & Stability Evaluation
Formulations undergo skin irritation patch testing (ISO 10993), comedogenicity assessment, shelf-life accelerated stability (40°C/75% RH for 6 months), microbiological challenge testing, and preservative efficacy testing. Dermatologically tested certifications require clinical panel assessments on 20+ volunteers.
07
🏭
Scale-Up & Filling
Approved formulations are scaled to 500–5,000 kg batches in jacketed stainless steel GMP-compliant reactors. Products are filled under nitrogen blanket to prevent oxidation, then sealed, labelled per BIS/EU/FDA regulations, and batch-released after QC sign-off.
08
✅
Regulatory Submission & Market Release
In India, sunscreens are regulated as cosmetics under the Drugs & Cosmetics Act via CDSCO. Products must comply with Schedule S and BIS IS 16248. Claims like "reef-safe," "broad-spectrum," and "hypoallergenic" require substantiated documentation before market release.
Dermatology

Effects on Skin

Sunscreens produce both beneficial photoprotective effects and, in some cases, adverse skin reactions depending on formulation and individual sensitivity.

☀️
Beneficial
UV Radiation Blocking
Broad-spectrum sunscreens (SPF 50+ PA++++) block UVB (290–320nm) causing sunburn, and UVA (320–400nm) causing photoaging. Physical filters reflect UV; chemical filters absorb and dissipate UV as heat, preventing DNA photodamage in keratinocytes.
🛡️
Beneficial
Prevention of Photoaging
Daily SPF use significantly reduces MMP (matrix metalloproteinase) activation that degrades collagen and elastin. Long-term studies show 24% reduction in visible aging markers. Antioxidants (Niacinamide, Vitamin C, E) boost photoprotection synergistically.
🎨
Beneficial
Hyperpigmentation Control
UV exposure triggers melanogenesis via MC1R pathway. Sunscreen prevents post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH) common in South Asian skin (Fitzpatrick III–V). Tinted formulations with Iron Oxides additionally block HEV light that exacerbates melasma.
🧬
Beneficial
Skin Cancer Prevention
Melanoma risk is reduced by 50% with regular SPF 15+ use (Australian AAD study). Broad-spectrum SPF 50+ is recommended for Indian outdoor workers. Photoprotection prevents UV-induced p53 gene mutations in basal keratinocytes.
⚠️
Adverse
Contact & Photoallergic Dermatitis
Benzophenone-3 (Oxybenzone) is the most common cause of sunscreen-induced photoallergy — affecting ~1–4% of users. Reactions manifest as erythema, vesicles, and pruritus in sun-exposed areas. Fragrance ingredients further increase sensitisation risk. Patch testing recommended for sensitive individuals.
🔴
Adverse
Acneform & Comedogenic Reactions
Heavy emollient bases (certain waxes, silicone systems) can occlude follicular ostia, triggering acneform eruptions in acne-prone skin. Dimethicone-heavy and some avobenzone-containing products are problematic for oily/combination skin. Non-comedogenic gel formulations are safer alternatives.
🧪
Adverse
Systemic Absorption (Chemical Filters)
FDA 2019 data showed plasma concentrations of Oxybenzone up to 209 ng/mL — far exceeding the 0.5 ng/mL safety threshold. Avobenzone, Octocrylene, and Homosalate also showed systemic absorption. Chronic endocrine disruption in hormonally sensitive populations remains a concern.
🌿
Beneficial
Barrier Enhancement (Select Formulations)
Formulations containing Ceramides (Kaya), Hyaluronic Acid (Dot & Key, Derma Co), Panthenol (Sebamed, Cetaphil), and Aloe Vera actively reinforce the skin barrier, reducing TEWL and improving microbiome balance — valuable for post-procedure or sensitive skin.
Pathology

Diseases & Disorders Associated with Sunscreen Chemicals

Certain UV filter chemicals have been linked to specific dermatological, systemic, and endocrine disorders — particularly with long-term or high-dose exposure.

Endocrine Disruption Syndrome
Chronic exposure to Oxybenzone and Octinoxate interferes with estrogen, androgen, and thyroid hormone pathways. Animal studies show altered reproductive organ development. Human epidemiology links high Oxybenzone urinary metabolites to reduced semen quality in men and early puberty onset in girls.
⚗ Oxybenzone (BP-3), Octinoxate (OMC), Homosalate
Photoallergic Contact Dermatitis
UV radiation transforms chemical filter molecules into reactive haptens that bind to skin proteins, triggering Type IV hypersensitivity reactions. Presents as persistent eczematous rash in sun-exposed areas, often misdiagnosed as sunburn. Can persist as chronic photosensitivity disorder requiring long-term avoidance.
⚗ Benzophenone-3, Octocrylene, PABA (older formulations)
Octocrylene-Induced Benzophenone Accumulation
Octocrylene undergoes in-product and in-skin hydrolysis to produce Benzophenone (BP), a Group 2B possible carcinogen per IARC. Studies detected elevated BP in blood, urine, and breast milk of regular Octocrylene sunscreen users. Several European countries have moved to restrict Octocrylene in products for children under 12.
⚗ Octocrylene → Benzophenone (metabolite)
Nano-TiO₂ Cytotoxicity & Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS)
Nanoscale Titanium Dioxide particles (<100nm) can generate reactive oxygen species (ROS) upon UV activation, potentially damaging cellular DNA and mitochondria. While intact skin provides an effective barrier, damaged or sun-burned skin may allow systemic entry. Inhalation risk from spray sunscreens with nano-TiO₂ is a separate occupational hazard.
⚗ Nano-grade Titanium Dioxide
Vitamin D Deficiency (Overcorrection Risk)
Very high SPF sunscreens used consistently over the entire body could theoretically reduce UVB-induced Vitamin D synthesis. However, real-world studies show most users apply insufficient quantities (average 0.5–1 mg/cm² vs recommended 2 mg/cm²), leaving significant UV exposure. Supplementation recommended for indoor workers using sunscreen on all exposed skin.
⚗ High-SPF formulations — indirect concern
Preservative Sensitivity & Parabens Link
Methylparaben (detected in some VLCC batches) has weak estrogenic activity and has been detected in human breast tumour tissue in small studies. Phenoxyethanol at concentrations >1% causes irritant contact dermatitis, particularly in infants and sensitive skin. EU restricts Phenoxyethanol to 1% in all leave-on products.
⚗ Methylparaben, Phenoxyethanol >1%

Case Study on Sunscreen — Indian Market Chemistry Analysis

This project investigates the cosmetic chemistry behind 20 commercially available Indian sunscreen products — analysing UV filter mechanisms, identifying harmful and beneficial ingredients, proposing safer formulation alternatives, and examining market trends. The research draws on Indian regulatory data (CDSCO), international studies (FDA, SCCS/EU), and clinical dermatology literature.
